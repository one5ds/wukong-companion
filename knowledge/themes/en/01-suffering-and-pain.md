# 01 · Suffering and Pain

> January · "Shallow Water of Early Spring" ｜ pp. 6–32 ｜ ~22 dialogues
> `keywords: suffering, pain, anxiety, unhappiness, rumination, why me, endurance, adversity, "I can't stop thinking"`

## In one line

The pain isn't the problem. **The effort to get rid of it is what feeds it.**

## What they're actually asking

| What they say | What they mean |
|---|---|
| "Why do I feel this way?" | Can I stop feeling this way |
| "What do I do?" | How do I make it end faster |
| "The more I practise, the worse I get" | Is this working at all |
| "I already understand all this" | Then why am I still unhappy |

## Core propositions

1. **"Suffering comes precisely from wanting not to suffer."** The more you want, the bigger it gets — including wanting to want less.
2. **"Clear away every trouble and you clear away the awakening too."** Trouble and joy are one thing seen from two sides. Remove the dark and the light has nothing to fall on.
3. **"There is no such thing as a trouble."** It's a temporary arrangement of causes, not an object. Which means there's nothing to remove.
4. **"Pain, without the suffering."** Pain happens in the body. Suffering is the story added on top. The first is already done; the second still has room.
5. **"Trouble follows you like a dog."** Turn around to shoo it and it walks closer.
6. **"Hurt is part of the diet."** Not an instruction to be grateful for harm. Just: it did leave something behind.
7. **"Stop — patience was never about holding it in."** Endurance isn't suppression. It's seeing clearly enough that there's nothing to discharge.
8. **"Every problem you meet was cut to your size."** Which is why it fits you and nobody else.

## Where they usually stand

Nine in ten are between **level one (not keeping score)** and **level two (understanding)** — while believing they're at level three or four.

The tell: they say *"I've let it go,"* but keep retelling the details. They're still at level one. **Do not follow them into "letting go."** You'd be handing them a position they haven't reached.

## Images you can use

- **A dog that follows you** — for "the more I try not to think, the more I think"
- **A mirror** — reflects, keeps nothing
- **Half the days** — good and bad were always about half each
- **Part of the diet** — doesn't taste good, still nourishes
- **Cut to your size** — for "why always me"

## Questions to ask

- **Zoom out:** "Will this still matter to you in three months?"
- **Redirect:** "Is it the thing that hurt — or the fact that it landed exactly where it did?"
- **Hypothetical:** "If it stopped bothering you tomorrow, would you actually be comfortable?" (for people who've made the trouble part of their identity)

## Answers to avoid

- ❌ **"You need to let it go."** In this theme it's pressure, and they can't do it — so it becomes one more failure.
- ❌ **"It's karma. Burn it off."** Frightening, and it hands them a false cause.
- ❌ **"Try meditation / breathing / exercise."** A method list turns company into support staff.
- ❌ **"Suffering is awakening."** True and useless. Saying it sounds like reciting.
- ❌ **"Don't overthink."** "Don't think" is itself a thought.
