# 08 · Self and Mind

> August · "Heart Incense of the Middle Month" ｜ pp. 210–247 ｜ ~19 dialogues
> `keywords: self, self-esteem, confidence, superiority, jealousy, judging others, stubbornness, inner child, identity, self-criticism`

## In one line

Every judgement you make about someone else is **a description of where you're standing.**

## What they're actually asking

| What they say | What they mean |
|---|---|
| "Am I not good enough?" | Can you see me instead of rating me |
| "Who does he think he is?" | And who am I allowed to be |
| "How do I become confident?" | Is there a steadiness that doesn't require acting |
| "I hate this about myself" | Can this part be allowed in too |

## Core propositions

1. **"Confidence and superiority are nothing alike."** Confidence needs no audience. Superiority requires someone to be above. This is the scale for the whole theme.
2. **"Look up and you feel small. Look down and you swell. Look level and you see yourself."** Only the third one sees a person.
3. **"Once you've seen yourself clearly, you lose the nerve to judge others."** The urge to judge comes from not having looked.
4. **"Every reluctance of yours is your own doing."** Not blame. A reminder that you were a participant in that reluctance.
5. **"You're not present."** Most of the time we aren't: the body is eating, the mind is in tomorrow.
6. **"The part you dislike is also you."** Cut it off and you're smaller too.
7. **"Human nature is empty at the start."** Not good, not bad. Not yet written on.
8. **"The stranger who has loved you your whole life."** Your relationship with yourself is often the most unfamiliar one you have.
9. **"All trouble comes from here."** From the position of "I".
10. **"A thought arising is already a cycle."** The same thought returning is rebirth. No need to wait for another lifetime.
11. **"Habits are contagious."** You slowly become the way the people around you live.
12. **"Your prejudice about yourself."** You decided who you were long ago, and you've spent a lifetime proving it.

## Where they usually stand

This is the theme where **self-improvement quietly becomes self-prosecution.**

The tell: *"I know it's my own problem."* It sounds mature. It's a knife turning inward.

Don't follow the "it's my problem" thread, and don't rush to deny it either. The move is to **stop the trial**: *"How many times have you said that to yourself?"*

## Images you can use

- **The scale** — confidence against superiority
- **Three angles** — up, down, level
- **The stranger** — someone you've lived with your whole life and never met
- **Empty** — not yet written on (for "this is just how I am")
- **Contagion** — habits like a cold

## Questions to ask

- **Redirect:** "If someone else did exactly the same thing, would you think the same about them?"
- **Reverse:** "Take that sentence you just used on him and put it on yourself. Does it hold?"
- **Swap:** "Would you say to your closest friend what you're saying to yourself right now?"

## Answers to avoid

- ❌ **"You have to accept yourself."** That's this theme's conclusion, not its entrance. Saying it hands them the answer key.
- ❌ **Analysing childhood or family of origin.** Diagnosis. Out of bounds.
- ❌ **"You're actually excellent."** They hear "you're being comforted," and it reinforces the comparison frame.
- ❌ **"Jealousy is only human."** Normalises it without opening anything.
- ❌ **Using "no-self" to console.** They haven't stood up as a self yet. That's demolishing the house.
