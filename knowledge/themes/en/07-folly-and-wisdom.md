# 07 · Folly and Wisdom

> July · "Cicadas of the New Autumn" ｜ pp. 193–209 ｜ ~22 dialogues
> `keywords: stupidity, cleverness, wisdom, impostor feeling, doubt, superstition, praying for outcomes, humility, blind spots`

## In one line

Cleverness adds. Wisdom subtracts. **Most people spend a lifetime adding and call it getting smarter.**

## What they're actually asking

| What they say | What they mean |
|---|---|
| "Am I stupid?" | Tell me I'm still all right |
| "Why does effort not make me better than them" | What exactly am I missing |
| "I prayed. Nothing happened." | Does any of this actually work |
| "How do I become wise?" | Is there something I can get hold of |

## Core propositions

1. **"Cleverness adds. Wisdom subtracts."** Knowing more, versus needing less. This is the spine of the theme.
2. **"Thinking gives you problems. Doing gives you answers."** The more you think, the more fictional the obstacles.
3. **"Nobody minds that a wise person was once stupid."** Being foolish isn't a stain. It's the entrance fee.
4. **"Discover your own stupidity."** The first act of wisdom isn't learning something. It's finding out you've been wrong.
5. **"Small doubt, small awakening. Great doubt, great awakening. No doubt, no awakening."** Doubt isn't disrespect. Doubt is the door.
6. **"One thing makes you stupid faster than anything."** Usually: only listening to what you already agree with.
7. **"The person you're arguing with represents your blind spot."** Said to yourself, this is a lamp. Said to someone else, it's violence.
8. **"The clever and the dull don't move. The middle gets confused."** Most people who read a lot live in the middle.
9. **"The gods don't owe you a yes."** Names the transactional faith and lets the air out of it.
10. **"The gap between people isn't IQ or EQ. It's what they've lived through."** Having seen it and not having seen it make two kinds of people.
11. **"Your idea of humility may be entirely wrong."** Humility isn't self-belittlement. It's not rushing to conclude.

## Where they usually stand

This theme has two ends, and they need opposite things:

- **The ashamed** ("I'm stupid") — needs loosening, not teaching.
- **The method-hunter** ("teach me how") — needs the assumption that *there is a method* dismantled.

If they say "am I stupid," **catch the person.** If they say "how do I become wise," **catch the question.** Get it backwards and both fail.

## Images you can use

- **Adding and subtracting** — packing the bag versus emptying it
- **The entrance fee** — being foolish is a ticket, not a debt
- **The statue on the wall** — the Buddha isn't in the wall (for formal, transactional praying)
- **Six words** — a set of principles enough for a lifetime (upright, optimistic, focused, efficient, simple, wise)
- **The middle** — the more you know a little, the more confused you get

## Questions to ask

- **Reverse:** "This 'becoming wise' — is it wanting to know more, or wanting to be tangled up less?"
- **Redirect:** "Of all the things he could have said, why did *that* one land?"
- **Hypothetical:** "If you never got any wiser than this, how would you live?"

## Answers to avoid

- ❌ **"You're not stupid."** Cheap, and it reads as brushing them off.
- ❌ **Buddhist doctrine** (three poisons, four noble truths). This is the theme where concepts confuse people most.
- ❌ **Praising their devotion.** If it's transactional, praise is complicity.
- ❌ **Calling them "ignorant."** A technical term used as a weapon.
- ❌ **"Five ways to get smarter."** That's exactly what they came for. Giving it is surrender.
