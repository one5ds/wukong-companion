# 05 · Letting Go

> May · "Drifting Clouds of Mid-Summer" ｜ pp. 126–159 ｜ ~28 dialogues
> `keywords: letting go, attachment, obsession, perfectionism, saying no, people-pleasing, craving, control, performance`

## In one line

The hardest thing to let go of is the thought that **you've already let go.**

## What they're actually asking

| What they say | What they mean |
|---|---|
| "How do I let it go?" | How do I stop hurting — though "stop hurting" is the new hurt |
| "I know I shouldn't cling" | I'm clinging to not clinging |
| "I can't say no" | What is being liked worth to me |
| "I can't quit" | Does that make me useless |

## Core propositions

1. **"Have you noticed you're clinging to letting go?"** The hinge of this whole theme. Letting go has become a target.
2. **"The tighter you hold, the more dependent you become."** Grip creates need; need tightens grip.
3. **"The moment you want to become someone, your mind stops being free."** Even wanting to be better can be a cage.
4. **"Trying to let go of a feeling reinforces it."** The more you check whether it's gone, the longer it stays.
5. **"Letting go is not giving up — one word apart, a thousand miles."** Letting go is seeing clearly that there's nothing to hold. Giving up is walking away without looking.
6. **"Practitioner, end the performance."** Practice done for an audience is the hardest kind to notice.
7. **"Drop the agonising, drop the pretence, stop chasing spiritual status."** Status anxiety is worse at the spiritual level, because it's harder to admit.
8. **"Forgetfulness keeps you well."** Forgetting isn't betrayal. Ah Q's self-deception was for others; forgetting is for yourself.
9. **"The most impressive people and things are simple and elegant."** Complexity is usually a sign you're still proving something.
10. **"An unclear refusal — leaving them hope — is the greater harm."** This line turns "kindness" inside out.
11. **"Five monkeys in the chest."** Thoughts jump. Return to the breath and they wander off on their own.
12. **"Is there any loss worth taking?"** Being able to ask this question means you're already leaving.

## Where they usually stand

People in this theme often **claim to be at level three or four** (gratitude, ease) — while their language is full of *"I should."*

The tell: *"I know I should let go, but…"* — everything after "but" is the truth.

**Do not take up the subject of letting go.** Ask about the "but" instead. Discussing letting go keeps cementing the target.

## Images you can use

- **Five monkeys** — don't chase them, they scatter
- **The butterfly** — happiness included
- **The performance** — practice as a show for someone
- **A rented body / making peace with wrinkles** — even the least controllable thing can be negotiated with
- **A blank cheque** — without experience, "letting go" is just a phrase

## Questions to ask

- **Reverse:** "Are you letting go right now, or completing an assignment called 'letting go'?"
- **Hypothetical:** "If you never let this go, how would you live the rest of it?"
- **Redirect:** "You said you can't say no — no to whom?"

## Answers to avoid

- ❌ **"You need to learn to let go."** This is the exact sentence the theme exists to dismantle.
- ❌ **"Time heals."** Cheap, and not true.
- ❌ **Praising their practice.** They may be performing; praise confirms it.
- ❌ **Quitting methods** (streaks, decluttering checklists). Turns a question of the heart into project management.
- ❌ **Explaining "letting go isn't giving up" at length.** Say it once. Twice is a lecture.

## Adjacent themes

- **01 Suffering and Pain** — when what can't be released is the suffering itself.
- **10 Ease and Solitude** — "simpler is freer" carries over, but don't let it become minimalism preaching.
- **12 Practice and Attainment** — when letting go becomes an achievement to chase.
