# 12 · Practice and Attainment

> December · "Dawn of the Closing Year" ｜ pp. 327–350 ｜ ~27 dialogues
> `keywords: practice, attainment, success, work, money, career, ambition, worldly life, effort, goals, shortcuts`

## In one line

Practice isn't an extra thing you do after stepping out of your life. **It's how you sit in a meeting, how you earn, how you deal with the difficult one.**

## What they're actually asking

| What they say | What they mean |
|---|---|
| "Do practice and earning money conflict?" | Am I too worldly |
| "I work hard and get nowhere" | Are effort and outcome even related |
| "How do I practise at work?" | What is the point of all this mess |
| "After all this practice, what did I get?" | Was it a waste |

## Core propositions

1. **"Treat work as practice and you're already most of the way there."** Not "the office is a temple" as a slogan. The specific impatience in the meeting is the material.
2. **"Worldly wisdom, ordinary life. Walking, sitting, all of it is practice. Everything you look at is the hall."** The summary line. No need to resign, no need for a mountain.
3. **"Money and practice."** They don't conflict. What conflicts is earning while believing it's dirty.
4. **"A life of practice is a solo one, unrelated to anyone else."** No audience. No opponent.
5. **"Life needs friends. Practice needs an 'enemy'."** The difficult one is the most effective curriculum. (**For self-reflection — not "be grateful to those who hurt you."**)
6. **"Practise without trying to get something out of it, or you get nothing."** Even wanting progress is wanting.
7. **"Practice isn't because you have great wisdom. It's because you have plenty of foolishness and trouble."** A low starting point isn't an obstacle. It's fuel.
8. **"Most shortcuts lead to mud."** Including a hundred books and ten courses.
9. **"The beggar in fine clothes."** Lofty inside, wrecked outside. The sharpest image in the theme.
10. **"The levels of practice."** Not certificates. The lag between being offended and your reaction.
11. **"An ordinary person's footprints."** Not fast. Still visible.
12. **"NPCs in your life."** Some people are only here to move your plot along. No need to hate them, no need to thank them.
13. **"Maybe a scorching life is your method."** You don't have to be cool and quiet.
14. **"Liberation isn't the goal. It's the familiarity inside the process."** Echoes November. **The process is the answer.**

## Where they usually stand

Two kinds here, and both drift easily:

- **The achiever** ("I want to succeed") → turns Zen into a productivity tool. The point: **don't make practice a second KPI.**
- **The spiritually superior** ("I don't care about that") → becomes the beggar in fine clothes. The point: **how their life actually runs says more than what they say.**

The tell: when they say "I don't care about money," ask *"then is this month's account settled?"* Not unkind. Just grounding.

## Images you can use

- **The hall** — the desk, the meeting room, the dinner table
- **NPCs** — roles that move the plot
- **Footprints** — slow, still there
- **The beggar** — dressed high, hands empty
- **Scorching** — a loud life can also be practice
- **Mud** — where shortcuts end
- **Fine clothes** — what you wear to look advanced

## Questions to ask

- **Redirect:** "You want to achieve something — if it never amounted to anything, would you still do it?"
- **Reverse:** "The person at work you can't stand — is there anything in him you'd rather not admit is in you?"
- **Narrow:** "What's one thing today you did fully present?"

## Answers to avoid

- ❌ **"Treat work as practice" followed by a long explanation.** Say it once. They've heard it.
- ❌ **"Be grateful to those who made it hard for you."** Moral coercion — especially under workplace bullying.
- ❌ **Talking about "vision" and "the long term."** Business jargon, nothing to do with this.
- ❌ **Denying their concern about money** ("money isn't what matters"). Dishonest, and easy from a safe seat.
- ❌ **Encouraging them to quit / ordain / walk away** to find "real practice." Extremely dangerous advice.

## A note on where the book ends

December is the closing month. By the time a reader gets here, one thing has usually become clear:

> The twelve months are not twelve stages. They are twelve doors. Any one of them opens onto the same place — **today.**
