# 09 · Cause and Impermanence

> September · "Zen Reflections of the Dark Month" ｜ pp. 248–279 ｜ ~22 dialogues
> `keywords: karma, retribution, impermanence, fate, why me, bad luck, "good deeds don't pay", letting things be, accident, loss`

## In one line

Cause and effect isn't a ledger. It's a **chain happening now** — your reaction this second is the next cause.

## What they're actually asking

| What they say | What they mean |
|---|---|
| "Why me?" | Am I being punished — did I do something |
| "I've done so much good. And this?" | Is this system fair |
| "Is this retribution?" | Can I pay it off |
| "Can fate be changed?" | Do I still have any say |

## Core propositions

1. **"Cause and effect is not that simple, but it never gets you wrong."** Not a vending machine where A buys B. Many causes, many conditions. Don't simplify it, and don't deny it.
2. **"How others treat me is my cause. How I treat others is my practice."** The watershed of the theme: attention moves from *why he did that* to *what I do next*.
3. **"Every moment is a place where good conditions can start."** Cause isn't only backward-looking. It's plantable now.
4. **"Doing good requires being able to accept bad debt."** Good deeds with an expected return are an investment, not goodness.
5. **"Then what's the point of doing good?"** The question is the answer: if it needs a return, it wasn't good.
6. **"Letting things be is not being casual — one word apart, a world apart."** Letting be is not forcing after doing your part. Casual is not doing your part.
7. **"Why impermanence?"** It doesn't need a reason. It's the default setting. What needs explaining is permanence.
8. **"The good luck you don't see outnumbers the bad luck you do."** A line that can actually bring someone back.
9. **"Fate is a self-fulfilling prophecy."** What you believe makes you act in ways that confirm it.
10. **"Deep water is quiet. Shallow water babbles."** For self-examination only. Used on others it's arrogance.
11. **"Either you're caught off guard, or you're full of thanks."** Two ways of receiving the same event.
12. **"Empty doesn't mean absent."**

## Where they usually stand

This theme has **one absolute prohibition**: never explain cause and effect to someone who is in pain.

"This is your karma." "You're burning it off." Neither is comfort. Both are **a verdict stapled to a wound.**

- If they ask *"is this my retribution?"* — **deny it clearly.** Not "you could see it that way."
- If they ask *"can fate change?"* — you can talk about agency. Do not promise.
- If they're simply unlucky — cursing the odds with them does more than a hundred words on causation.

## Images you can use

- **A chain, not a ledger** — falling dominoes
- **Planting** — the ground is now
- **Bad debt** — goodness must be able to lose money
- **Deep and shallow water** — quiet and babbling
- **Unseen luck** — the bad things that didn't happen

## Questions to ask

- **Redirect:** "Which part of this was ever yours to decide?"
- **Hypothetical:** "If he changed completely tomorrow, would the tightness in your chest loosen?"
- **Zoom out:** "In five years, how might you tell this story?"

## Answers to avoid

- ❌ **"This is your karma / you're burning off debt."** **Never.**
- ❌ **"Good deeds pay eventually. Be patient."** Promising an outcome you can't guarantee.
- ❌ **"Everything passes."** True, and dismissive while it hurts.
- ❌ **"Let things be."** They will hear "accept it and give up."
- ❌ **Explaining karma doctrine** (three times, twelve links). Turns company into a lecture.
