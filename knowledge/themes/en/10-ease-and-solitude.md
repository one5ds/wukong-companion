# 10 · Ease and Solitude

> October · "Contemplation of the First Winter Month" ｜ pp. 280–302 ｜ ~19 dialogues
> `keywords: loneliness, freedom, exhaustion, ease, simplicity, roles, being alone, burnout, social life, protection`

## In one line

You're not tired because there's too much to do. You're tired because **too many things have permission to move your mood.**

## What they're actually asking

| What they say | What they mean |
|---|---|
| "Why am I so tired?" | Is there any part of me left that's mine |
| "I'm afraid of being alone" | When no one's here, who am I |
| "I want freedom" | Can I be free of responsibility |
| "I play too many roles" | Which one is actually me |

## Core propositions

1. **"You're tired because too many things can move your mood."** Not workload. Permissions. Everyone has edit access to your day.
2. **"Loneliness is the ordinary state of a life."** Not an aberration. Treating it as an aberration is what makes it panic.
3. **"Sign a decent agreement with loneliness."** Don't eliminate it. Don't be ruled by it. Negotiate terms.
4. **"Whatever protects you also limits you."** The stability, the relationship, the title — each gives safety and takes something in exchange.
5. **"Simpler is freer."** Subtract until you can't, and what's left is yours.
6. **"The more roles, the less ease."** Every role comes with expectations. Too many coats is simply heavy.
7. **"I only ask how much ease you have."** A single measure: not attainments, ease.
8. **"No high or low dharma — only a big or small heart."** Capacity matters more than method.
9. **"You were born with wings. Why do you like to crawl?"** Sharp. **Do not say this to someone in a low place.**
10. **"Pain without speaking. Laughter without explaining."** Not suppression. Simply not owing anyone an account.
11. **"Where is the other shore?"** Whoever asks this is usually already looking elsewhere.
12. **"Don't tell me what you believe."** Ease requires no credentials.

## Where they usually stand

Two kinds of people, and they need opposite handling:

- **The overloaded** ("I'm exhausted") → needs **subtraction**: find the one thing to put down. Exactly one.
- **The escaping** ("I just want to be alone") → needs **discernment**: is solitude recovery or hiding? Recovery comes back. Hiding doesn't want to.

Ask: *"When was the last time you felt light?"* If the answer is "a long time ago," it's probably the second.

## Images you can use

- **Permissions** — who has edit access to your mood
- **The agreement** — a contract with loneliness
- **Wings and crawling** — **use with care**
- **Coats** — too many roles
- **One coin** — protection and limitation on the same face

## Questions to ask

- **Redirect:** "What's one thing today you did because you wanted to, not because you were pushed?"
- **Hypothetical:** "If nobody would ever know how you're doing, how would you spend this month?"
- **Narrow:** "Is there half a day this week that's entirely yours?"

## Answers to avoid

- ❌ **"Learn to enjoy being alone."** Insulting to someone currently in pain from it.
- ❌ **"Get out more, meet people."** Treating introversion as an illness.
- ❌ **"You were born with wings" and similar.** A second injury during a low period.
- ❌ **"Just put some things down."** They can't, and it's too abstract to help.
- ❌ **Romanticising solitude as "advanced."** They'll use it as permission to hide.
